The 80% Trap

AI tools are brilliant at getting you 80% of the way there.

That last 20%? That's where your business actually lives.

An AI-built store handles "add to cart" beautifully. Mine does too. But what happens when:

  • You need different tax rates for physical goods vs. digital downloads in the same order?
  • You want "buy one, get one 50% off" but only for email subscribers and only on Wednesdays?
  • A customer has a coupon, a loyalty discount, and free shipping—and the math needs to work perfectly every time?

AI starts guessing. And when AI guesses wrong, your checkout breaks and you lose sales.

I don't guess. I build.

The Digital Landlord Problem

AI platforms are walled gardens. You're not building a store. You're renting one.

The code lives on their servers. The rules are their rules. If they change pricing next year, you pay. If they deprecate a feature you rely on, you adapt. If they go out of business, your store vanishes overnight.

A custom-built store is yours. Every line of code. You can host it anywhere. Take it to any developer. No platform holds your business hostage.

You don't rent a store from me. You own it.

Speed Isn't Just About Building—It's About Loading

AI tools optimize for their speed. How fast can they generate something?

Google optimizes for your speed. How fast does your site load for a customer?

Here's what happens inside AI builders: They dump heavy JavaScript onto your pages because it's easier to generate. Your store looks good on your screen, but in the real world—on a customer's spotty 4G connection—it crawls.

Here's what I do: I build for load speed first. Every image optimized. Every script trimmed. Every line of code pulling its weight.

Google ranks faster sites higher. Customers buy from faster sites. It's that simple.

The Maintenance Nightmare Nobody Talks About

Let's talk about six months from now.

🔻 Scenario A: AI‑built store

Something breaks. Maybe a browser updates. Maybe Stripe changes their API. Maybe the AI platform pushes an update that accidentally breaks your layout. Now what?

You spend hours in a chat bot, typing increasingly desperate prompts, trying to explain the problem to an AI that has long since forgotten how the first 80% of your store works.

Or you hire a developer to untangle the mess—which costs more than building it right the first time.

✅ Scenario B: Built by me

Something breaks? You email me. I fix it. You go back to running your business.

You don't learn debugging. You don't become a prompt engineer. You sell things. I handle the code.

That's the deal.
